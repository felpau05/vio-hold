BENCH PROCEDURE: CAN THE BOARD READ THE FORWARD CAMERA, AND DOES THE READ DISTURB THE OTHER VIDEO?
Event day. Propellers off. Written 2026-11-21. Not done yet on the real aircraft.

WHY THIS TEST
The forward camera (Justice A) is the pilot's flight camera. The pilot sees its picture in the goggles.
The board's CSI-2 input is shared between a digital camera and an analog decoder chip.
A device-tree tool (orqa_dt_switcher) and a restart select which one the board reads.
Our checklist forbids orqa_dt_switcher today.
A blocking analog read can delay the digital capture. This procedure measures it.
This procedure finds out three things:
  1. Can the board read the forward camera with the device tree that is on the board now?
  2. Does that read disturb the nadir camera (Justice D) capture of our program?
  3. Does that read disturb the pilot's video?

CONDITIONS BEFORE YOU START
- The pilot agrees to this test. The forward camera is the pilot's camera. If the pilot does not agree, do not do this test.
- The crew lead agrees to this test.
- The propellers are off the aircraft.
- The pilot holds the goggles and looks at the picture during the test.
- Nobody runs orqa_dt_switcher. Nobody restarts the board with another device tree.
- Nobody disconnects or splits a cable of the pilot's camera.

STOP RULES
- If a step needs orqa_dt_switcher, a device-tree change, or a cable change, stop. Record "not readable today".
- If the pilot reports any change in the goggles picture, stop fwd_rec at once (Ctrl+C). Record FAIL.
- If the nadir camera stops, stop fwd_rec at once. Record FAIL.

PART A. FIND THE DEVICES (read only, no change)
A1. Ask the pilot to look at the goggles picture for 30 s. Record what the pilot sees (brightness, lines, OSD).
A2. On the board, list the video devices:
      v4l2-ctl --list-devices
      ls -l /dev/video*
A3. For each device, record the driver, the input name, the standard and the picture size:
      v4l2-ctl -d /dev/videoN --all
A4. Find the nadir camera device. It is the device in video_device of settings.json.
A5. Find an analog decoder device. It reports a standard (PAL or NTSC) and a size of 720x576 or 720x480.
PASS RULE A: an analog decoder device exists now, AND the nadir camera device also exists now.
If PASS RULE A is not true, stop. Record "forward camera not readable without a device-tree change". Do not do part B.

PART B. READ TEST (only when PASS RULE A is true)
B1. Start the program in bench mode for 60 s. This is the baseline. It writes logs/run_*.csv:
      ~/.venvs/vio/bin/python apb_main.py bench --height 1.5
B2. Stop the bench run after 60 s. Keep its run log as BASELINE.
B3. Start the bench run again. Keep this run log as WITH_FWD.
B4. In a second terminal, start the forward recorder for 60 s. Use the analog decoder device from A5:
      nice -n 10 ~/.venvs/vio/bin/python tools/fwd_rec.py --device /dev/videoN --hz 5 --seconds 60
B5. While B4 starts and stops, ask the pilot to look at the goggles. The decoder can reset when a device opens.
B6. Wait until fwd_rec prints "stopped (time is up)". Stop the bench run.
B7. Start fwd_rec again with no time limit. After 20 s, push Ctrl+C. Record the last line that it prints.
B8. Start fwd_rec again for 10 s. This shows that the device is free after Ctrl+C.
B9. Compare the nadir frame times of BASELINE and WITH_FWD (column t of the run log):
      ~/.venvs/vio/bin/python -c "import csv,sys,numpy as n
      for p in sys.argv[1:]:
       t=n.array([float(r['t']) for r in csv.DictReader(open(p)) if r['t']]);d=n.diff(t)*1e3
       print(p,len(t)/(t[-1]-t[0]),'fps, gap p50',n.median(d),'p99',n.percentile(d,99),'max',d.max(),'ms')" BASELINE.csv WITH_FWD.csv
B10. Open one forward picture and look at it:
      ~/.venvs/vio/bin/python -c "import numpy as n,cv2,glob;d=n.load(sorted(glob.glob('logs/fwd_*.npz'))[-1]);cv2.imwrite('/tmp/fwd.png',d['imgs'][0])"

PASS RULES FOR PART B (all must be true)
B-FWD   fwd_rec kept 285 to 315 pictures in 60 s (5 each second). It dropped 0 chunks. The picture shows the
        scene, not a black or flat screen.
B-NADIR The nadir frame rate of WITH_FWD is within 2 % of BASELINE. The largest gap between nadir frames is not
        more than the BASELINE largest gap plus one frame time (17 ms at 60 fps). The run log has no "no frame" rows.
B-PILOT The pilot says that the goggles picture did not change at any time: no flicker, no loss of the picture,
        no change of brightness, no lines. This includes the start and the stop of fwd_rec.
B-STOP  After Ctrl+C, fwd_rec prints "stopped (Ctrl+C)", the last chunk file exists, and step B8 works.
B-CPU   fwd_rec uses less than 15 % of one core (read it in top during B4).

RECORD
Record each PASS or FAIL, the devices from A3 and the numbers from B9 on the bring-up record sheet.
If any rule fails, do not run fwd_rec near a flight today.
If all rules pass, and the pilot and the crew lead agree, fwd_rec can record during a normal flight.
After the flight, run tools/fwd_check.py on the recording. That is a check only. It does not change the hold.
