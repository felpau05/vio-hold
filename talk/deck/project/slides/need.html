Holding position is what keeps the mission alive: eyes stay on the target, the pilot works the camera instead of fighting drift, the drone does not slide into danger, and it works under bridges and indoors where GNSS never reaches.
And Sidro needs no new hardware: it can ship as a software update to M2-AIs already flying, and to other drones with a downward camera and a small Linux board.

One minute: first the need, then the value. Say the sources. 220 percent is IATA flight data. The middle card: market leaders recognise the need. Quantum Systems, a leading maker of reconnaissance drones, added optical navigation to its Vector drones in Ukraine (trade-press report of a company announcement, 2024). If asked how many: Kyiv had bought more than 400 Vector systems, each a high-end aircraft. Lockheed: its Indago 4 page lists GPS-denied navigation using its Northstar software (checked on lockheedmartin.com by the team, 2026-11-21). Canada: the Defence Drone Initiative lists low-cost tactical ISR drones first of six use cases; the page does not mention GNSS, so do not claim it does. If asked for a source on position hold: the National Academies say losing GNSS "may eliminate or reduce the airframe's ability to hold a certain position and altitude". The four uses in the bold line are our assessment. Then the business line: every team has the same hardware, so the value is that the result ships as an update to aircraft already flying. Do not say "75 percent of drones lost to jamming": not verified. If asked about other drones: it needs a downward camera, an IMU and a small Linux board; porting means new device names, a lens calibration and a clock check. Only one M2-AI has been measured so far.
