== Paper sent by Paul, 2026-11-16 ==
LRPL-VIO: A Lightweight and Robust Visual-Inertial Odometry with Point and Line Features
Sensors 2024, 24(4), 1322. Nankai University. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10892506/
Read in full.

What it is: a full (tightly coupled) VIO. One filter holds position, speed, orientation, IMU biases, the
IMU-to-camera time shift and a window of past poses. It tracks corner points AND line segments.
Tested on handheld and indoor data sets (EuRoC, UMA-VI, VIODE) and one indoor rig (RealSense D455 at 30 Hz,
Xsens IMU at 200 Hz). Not about hover, height, a downward camera, wind, control or an autopilot.
Numbers: mean trajectory error 0.162 m on EuRoC (VINS-Mono 0.192, HybVIO 0.183). 30.5 ms per frame on a
laptop Core i7 (PL-VINS: 93 ms). No code or data published. Rolling shutter: not mentioned.

Not usable as a whole for us: needs a fast, well-timed IMU on the Linux side; 30 ms on an i7 is about 10
times too slow for the drone's processor; no code; a hover gives a one-camera VIO little motion for scale.

Ideas to borrow:
1. Estimate the camera-to-IMU time shift all the time, as part of the estimate (their Eq. 1), not only once
   in a calibration. Timing is our largest error source (10 to 20 ms doubled the drift).
2. Line features for low-texture scenes. From above, path edges, road edges and roof lines stay sharp when
   corners are few. A second route for the grass problem, beside the whole-picture match.
3. Stationary detection with a zero-speed update (their Eq. 27). Fits the "zero error" goal: when the picture
   shows no motion, tell the filter that the speed is zero (we would send speed to the autopilot).
4. Pick a subset of the tracked features per update to keep the compute low (their Eqs. 7 to 10).
